# Native accessibility — M0-12 (20 September 2026)

CDP probe on the real application, native Windows Muse sidecar 1.3.0, `main` commit after #161 was merged.
These measurements concern **Windows/WebView2** only.

## Procedure

```powershell
$env:WEBVIEW2_ADDITIONAL_BROWSER_ARGUMENTS = "--remote-debugging-port=9222"
npm run tauri -- dev
node scripts/cdp-a11y-probe.mjs
node scripts/cdp-focus-pixels.mjs --out <folder>
```

## 1. Effective tab order

24 presses of `Tab` through `Input.dispatchKeyEvent` (real keystrokes, not synthetic events), `document.activeElement` recorded after each.

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| Stops | **24** |
| Distinct stops | **24** — no focus trap, no loop |
| Order | logical: `Latest messages` → **composer** → `Start voice input` → authorization mode → reasoning effort → `Model settings` → `Skip to message input` → `Collapse sidebar` → navigation → sidebar |
| With no visible indicator | **1** — the `TEXTAREA` itself (see §3) |
| Carrying `aria-keyshortcuts` | 1 — `DIV.stream` with **`Home End PageUp PageDown`** |

The order follows the DOM coherently: conversation content first, then navigation, then the conversation list. No non-interactive element is reached.

## 2. The Ctrl+F shortcut

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| State before | finder **visible** (`Find in conversation` present in the body) |
| After `Ctrl+F` | finder **closed**, focus moved to a search field |
| The focused field's `placeholder` | **`Search messages…`** |
| The focused field's `aria-label` | **`Search messages`** |

**Established:** `Ctrl+F` is indeed wired and **toggles** the finder; focus goes to the search field, which carries an accessible label. The finder being open before the measurement, the effect observed is a closing: the test proves the shortcut's binding, not its opening from a closed state.

## 3. Focus indicator — a false positive corrected

First measurement: a narrow crop **around the `TEXTAREA` alone** gave two screenshots with **identical SHA-256**, which suggested the absence of any focus indicator. The computed styles confirmed that reading (`outline: none 0px`, `box-shadow: none` on the textarea **and** on `.composer-main`, including with `:focus-visible` active).

**That conclusion was false.** The field is transparent and borderless: the indicator is drawn on the **container** `.composer`, outside the initial crop. Widening the region to the whole footer:

| Crop | No focus | With focus | Identical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrow (textarea alone) | `8ef73afb…` | `8ef73afb…` | **yes** — invalid measurement |
| Wide (footer) | `b6e715c1…` | `1d17edba…` | **no** |

The wide screenshot shows a **blue ring** around the composer when the field has focus.

**Established:** the composer **has a visible focus indicator**. **Methodological correction to keep:** measuring a transparent field's focus requires cropping its container, not the field — otherwise you wrongly conclude it is absent.

*Reservation:* the wide screenshot crops the top of the composer and the ring is truncated there. Both images will be re-cropped wider for a complete proof.

## 5. `forced-colors` and `prefers-contrast: more` — emulated and measured

These two blocks only activate if the platform requests them; they had **never been exercised**. They are here, through `Emulation.setEmulatedMedia`, **without changing anything on the machine**: the emulation lives in the page session.

### `@media (forced-colors: active)` — honoured

| Control | `outline` (focus) | `border` | `color` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `button` | `rgb(26, 235, 255)` 2px | white | white |
| `a[href]` | `rgb(26, 235, 255)` 2px | **yellow** | **yellow** |
| `textarea` | `rgb(26, 235, 255)` 2px | cyan | white |
| `input` | `rgb(26, 235, 255)` 2px | white | white |
| `summary` | `rgb(26, 235, 255)` 2px | **yellow** | **yellow** |

The colours are the ones the engine injects in **Windows high contrast**, not the Muse palette. The three declarations are therefore effective: `outline-color: Highlight` (cyan, five controls), `border-color: ButtonText` (white, resolved to cyan on `textarea`), `color: LinkText` (yellow on `a` **and** `summary`).

### `@media (prefers-contrast: more)` — applied, except for one rule

The media does activate in the page (`matchMedia` → `true`), and a **witness rule injected** into the same media responds to the emulation (`rgb(1,2,3)`), which establishes that the emulation reaches this webview's style recalculation.

**But the file's `.muted { color: var(--text) }` rule does not apply:**

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Visible `.muted` elements | 9 |
| `--text` resolved on the element | `#e9eff3` |
| `--muted` resolved on the element | `#92a2ad` |
| Computed colour, emulation **off** / **on** | `rgb(146, 162, 173)` — **unchanged** |
| Witness rule `.project-group-header.muted` in the same media | **applied** |
| Manual injection of `.muted { color: var(--text) }` | `rgb(233, 239, 243)` — **applied** |

**Cause:** on an element carrying **both** `project-group-header` and `muted`, two rules of **equal specificity (0,1,0)** compete for `color`:

| Rule | Line | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `.muted, small` | `App.css:133` | `var(--muted)` |
| `.muted` under `@media (prefers-contrast: more)` | `App.css:2838` | `var(--text)` |
| `.project-group-header` | `App.css:3193` | `var(--muted)` |

At equal specificity, the **last declared** wins: **3193 beats 2838**. The high-contrast rule is **inoperative on this surface**.

### A fix attempt, and why it failed

I tried raising the media rule to **two classes** (`:where(.app) .muted`, `:where()` having zero specificity). The edit was on disk, and the stylesheet loaded in the browser did contain `:where(.app) .muted -> var(--text)` — verified after a **full restart** of the application to rule out any HMR cache.

**The computed colour did not change.** The measured specificity ladder explains why: a rule **injected last** wins even with **a single** class.

| Selector injected, under the same media | Classes | Applied? |
|---|---|---|
| `.muted` | 1 | **yes** |
| `.project-group-header.muted` | 2 | yes |
| `.app .project-group-header.muted` | 3 | yes |
| `.app .sidebar .project-group-header.muted` | 4 | yes |
| `#__probe .project-group-header.muted` | 5 | no (not applicable, the id does not exist) |

And the decisive test: the **file's exact selector** (`:where(.app) .muted`), re-injected with a literal colour, **wins** (`rgb(33,33,33)`). The same declaration, left in place in `App.css`, **does not win**.

**Conclusion: specificity is not the deciding factor.** What decides is the **declaration position** in the final cascade. My two successive hypotheses — "three rules at (0,1,0)" then "raising to (0,2,0) is enough" — were therefore **insufficient**, and the second was disproved by measurement.

**Fix withdrawn.** `src/App.css` was restored to its original state: I cannot validate that a fix repairs anything until the exact mechanism is understood. An unverified fix would be worse than no fix.

**Lead for later:** move the `@media (prefers-contrast: more)` block **after** the component styles — at the end of the stylesheet, or into `Desktop.css`, loaded second. That is consistent with a rule injected last winning. Not attempted in this pass.

**No fix was applied**: `src/` is unchanged, the defect is recorded with its reproduction and the detail of the failure.

## What stays open for M0-12

- **A real screen reader**: not exercised. No screen reader was driven; the roles and labels observed (`aria-label`, `aria-keyshortcuts`, `placeholder`) are a necessary condition, not proof of correct announcement.
- **A complete mouse-free path**: 24 stops recorded, the scenario not carried through.
- **The `prefers-contrast` defect above**: to fix in the code.
- **macOS / Linux**: outside this pass's scope.

**M0-12 is therefore not closed.** Forced contrast now has measurements — `forced-colors` is honoured, `prefers-contrast` activates but one of its two intentions stays without effect.
