## What the scenario adds

The profile declared only **one project** (`openscreen`), although the sessions already covered two workspaces. The "Start in" picker therefore offered a single root, and the "two projects" scenario in M0-01's text was not reproducible.

A **second project** was declared in `muse-desktop.projects.v1`, pointing at `C:\Users\etien\Documents\repos\muse-desktop`, with the user's explicit agreement. The script only mutates that state under `--declare`.

### The isolation test

The second project's host (`PID 35576`) was killed. State recorded **in the same application**, with no restart:

| Conversation | Project | State after the host died |
|---|---|---|
| "Reply with just the word ISOLATED and nothing else." | muse-desktop | **`Disconnected`** — "host process ended. Reconnect to continue." |
| "Enumerate the three Musketeers by name." | openscreen | **`Connected`** |

Other measurements at the same moment: **64 sessions preserved**, application `Responding: True`, remaining hosts `46932` and `18764`, **no respawn**.

## Conclusion

**Established:**

- **one host per workspace**: opening a conversation in a second project creates a third `muse serve` process;
- a host's death **affects only the conversations of its workspace**; conversations in other projects stay `Connected`;
- the application does not restart, loses no session and silently relaunches no host.

**Not established — and I do not present it as secured:**

## A turn carried to completion on A while B died

The remaining criterion was "A keeps working **while** B dies", not merely "A works afterwards". The measurement was redone in that configuration.

1. the `openscreen` conversation (A) receives a long turn — "Count slowly from one to sixty, one number per line";
2. the `muse-desktop` conversation (B) is **reconnected**, which recreates its host: **`PID 12264`, 15:35:05**;
3. A is confirmed working: `working: true`, `Running`, `stale: false`, `connected: true`;
4. B's host is killed (`Stop-Process 12264`) **while A is working**;
5. A is observed every 12 s.

| Moment | Remaining hosts | State of A |
|---|---|---|
| Right after the kill | `46932`, `18764` | `working: true`, `connected: true`, **`stale: false`**, `completed: 3` |
| +12 s | 2 | `working: false`, `completed: 3`, `connected: true` |
| +24 s / +36 s / +48 s | 2 | stable, `connected: true`, `stale: false` |

**Established:** A **was working at the moment** B's host was killed, and A **carried its turn to completion** — moving from `working: true` to `working: false` with 3 `Completed` sub-agents, **without ever going stale or asking to reconnect**. The application stayed alive and no host was silently relaunched.

That is M0-01's "A keeps working when B dies" criterion, observed directly.

### Useful method details

- Submitting in the composer is done through an **`Enter` keyboard event** on the `<textarea>` ("Enter to send"). A `clickText("Send")` fails: the send button is an icon with no usable label.
- **No automatic respawn.** B's host, killed in the previous pass, was not relaunched: its conversation stayed `Disconnected`, composer disabled, until an explicit click on **Reconnect**. Expected behaviour, and verified.

## What remains unestablished

- **No concurrent approvals**: an explicit M0-01 criterion, still open. The profile's authorization mode is `yolo` and the host's ceiling is `promptUnmatched`; no approval card was presented in these turns.
- The host's death comes from a `Stop-Process` on a PID identified by its timestamp, not from a controlled shutdown from the interface.
- A's turn was observed through to `working: false`, but **with no terminal notification from the host**: the ending is inferred from the interface's state, host 1.3.0 not emitting `turn/completed`.

## Attempt at concurrent approvals — not obtained

M0-01's last criterion: two concurrent approval requests, one per project. **No approval request could be provoked**, and I document the failure rather than work around it.

What was done, driving the real DOM:

1. **The authorization mode was indeed switched** from `yolo` to `ask` through the interface's control. Verified on three concurring sources: `muse-desktop.authorization-mode.v1` goes from `"yolo"` to `"ask"`, the `Ask for approval` option becomes `aria-checked="true"` (the other two `false`), and the composer's pill shows **"Ask for approval"**. The mode was then **restored to `yolo`**.
2. **A turn using a tool** ("Read the file README.md and tell me its first heading"): finished in ~24 s, **no approval card**, no `Allow`/`Deny` button.
3. **A turn targeting the network** ("Run this exact shell command… `curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://example.com`"), with the network in `sandbox-network restricted`: **no approval card** either. The turn proceeded normally, with a spell of stale then a return to a stable state.

**Conclusion:** `ask` mode is correctly **displayed and persisted** by the client, but **no request was presented** under these conditions. The host's ceiling is `promptUnmatched`: the actions tested are therefore presumably considered **covered by the matching** and approved without a question. Triggering a request would require an unmatched action, which I did not identify within this campaign's scope.

**Consequence for M0-01:** the "concurrent approvals" criterion **stays open**. The non-contamination evidence obtained elsewhere — two simultaneous hosts, B's death with no effect on A, A's turn carried to completion while B died — does not replace it.
